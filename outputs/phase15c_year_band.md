## The period-coverage confound, and why B1 survives it while B2 does not

15b's TRUE patterns are built from Ur III-era year-name formulae (the royal names are the Ur III dynasty's; the event verbs are its year-name conventions). The LOWER bound is therefore a floor, not an estimate, in every non-Ur III period — early and Old Babylonian year formulae that a specialist would call TRUE land in UNCLEAR. This cuts differently per verdict:

- **B1 is conservative despite the confound.** Its strong form compares Ur III's LOWER bound (understates Ur III) against Lagash II's UPPER bound (overstates Lagash II — UNCLEAR needs no TRUE-pattern coverage). Both biases run AGAINST the jump, and it still holds: 63.0% vs 19.8%. The jump is real under any resolution of UNCLEAR.
- **B2 is unadjudicable, not refuted.** The band direction flips across bounds (lower: OB 40.0% < Ur III 63.0%; upper: 85.4% > 72.8%), and the lower bound is exactly where the OB coverage gap bites. The rise-into-OB reading stays descriptive and provisional (n=130); it is not withdrawn as false, it is withdrawn as a band-supported claim.
- B4's only unstable step is Ur III → Old Babylonian, the same confound.

Whatever survives here remains PROBE-CONTINGENT (the probe itself failed its audit); what fails here is not asserted as a shape claim. The seal-based results (S1, the 19x sealing jump) are untouched — they rest on gold-audited probes.

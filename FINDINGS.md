# Findings

What this repository actually establishes, in one place, with the evidence for each claim and the
reason it can be believed. Ordered by strength. Every number here is regenerated by the pipeline —
nothing in this file is asserted by hand.

If you are checking whether to trust this work, read this file and [CORRECTIONS.md](CORRECTIONS.md).
Together they are the complete honest account.

## 1. `<RULING>` marks fall where cross-boundary similarity is LOWER

**Claim.** In Ur III administrative tablets, adjacent ruling-delimited chunks share significantly
*fewer* trigrams than the same text cut at alternative positions with an identical chunk-length
profile.

**Evidence.** Full corpus (91,606 tablets), 2,095 adjacent chunk pairs for Administrative, 10,000
permutations, two independent nulls, Benjamini–Hochberg corrected across genres.
Δ = −0.328 against the length-permutation null, BH-adjusted two-sided p = 0.0006.
Literary (248 pairs) and Royal Inscription (64 pairs) show the same direction and a comparable
effect size but are underpowered. See [`outputs/phase4_ruling_fullcorpus.md`](outputs/phase4_ruling_fullcorpus.md).

**Why it can be believed.** Two nulls with different weaknesses agree on direction. The primary null
preserves the chunk-length distribution exactly, so the effect is not a chunk-size artifact. The
known degeneracy of that null (tablets whose chunk lengths are all equal cannot move) affects only
1.2% of tablets and biases *against* finding an effect. Effect sizes carry bootstrap CIs.

**Mechanism — now tested directly (Phase 6).** In Administrative tablets, transaction-*closing*
formulae are strongly enriched in the line immediately before a ruling: `šu ba-ti` ("received")
**6.31×** enriched (p = 0.0001) and `kišib₃` (seal) **2.52×** (p = 0.0001), under a null that holds
each line's relative position in its tablet fixed and permutes labels only within a position decile.
The obvious objection — that rulings and closing formulae might merely co-cluster toward the back of
a tablet — is therefore ruled out: controlling for position does not weaken the effect, it slightly
strengthens it (5.40× → 6.31×). Date elements are *depleted* — as expected, since
in Ur III practice the date closes the whole tablet rather than each internal entry, and tablet-final
lines are excluded by design. A letter-*opening* formula used as a negative control shows nothing.
So a ruling separates completed transactions, and an arbitrary cut lands mid-record leaving related
material on both sides — which is exactly why arbitrary cuts show higher overlap. See
[`outputs/phase6_ruling_mechanism.md`](outputs/phase6_ruling_mechanism.md).

**This also addresses the editorial-artifact concern.** Ruling placement is predicted by the semantic
content of the preceding line. A modern editor drawing lines without regard to content could not
produce a 5.4× enrichment of `šu ba-ti` at p = 0.0001.

**What it still does not establish.** Whether this generalises beyond Ur III administrative practice
(Literary is underpowered, other genres lack record structure), or whether the association reflects
scribal intent rather than a downstream regularity of layout. The Phase 4 effect is also modest in
size: a ~22% reduction against a mean of 1.19 shared trigrams per pair.

**Relationship to the withdrawn claim.** The original release claimed the *opposite* direction
(chunks share *more*) on an invalid null, and that claim stays withdrawn. This is an independent,
adequately powered result that happens to support the same design intuition by the reverse mechanism.
Getting the right answer for the wrong reason is not a vindication, and it is not being treated as one.

## 2. Sealed and dated administrative writing is common but not universal

**Claim.** 25.4% of administrative tablets carry a `kišib₃` (seal) clause; 70.2% carry a year-name;
15.2% carry `šu ba-ti` ("received"). 23.2% carry neither seal, year-name, nor witness clause.

**Evidence.** Regex probes over a 2,069-tablet stratified sample, each precision-audited:
`seal_of_PN` 96%, `received_by` 100%, strict year-name probe. See
[`outputs/probe_validation.md`](outputs/probe_validation.md).

**Why it can be believed.** These four probes were partitioned match-by-match against their claimed
semantic role and validated at 95–100% precision. Prevalence is reported per genre.

**What it does not establish.** Anything about *importance*. An earlier version claimed "no important
write is anonymous, undated, or unattributed" — that is false in this sample and is withdrawn.
Whether unsealed tablets were less consequential is not something a regex can answer.

## 3. Genres differ in structural redundancy, and the ranking survives a length control

**Claim.** Royal Inscription > Administrative > Literary/Letter > Lexical in compression-based
structural redundancy (Δ +0.05 to +0.10 versus a token-shuffled baseline).

**Evidence.** zlib ratio on real versus shuffled token streams, re-verified on equal-length
*contiguous* blocks. See `outputs/compression_findings.md` §2.

**Why it can be believed.** The comparison was tested for the stream-length confound that
invalidated the Zipf claim, and survives it. The token-shuffle baseline is legitimate *here* because
the claim is only "more structured than random token order" — which is exactly what that baseline
tests, unlike the RULING case where it was smuggled in to support a much stronger claim.

**What it does not establish.** That any genre is a "domain-specific language". That was the original
overreach. Compression Δ is a descriptive contrast, not a test of grammar or of any specific structure.

## 4. Token-frequency distributions are heavy-tailed, and that is all that can be said

**Claim.** At a common stream length, MLE exponents fall in α ≈ 1.74–1.98 across all five genres. A
power law is ruled out for Lexical (bootstrap GoF p = 0.003) and not ruled out for the rest — but
Vuong's test cannot distinguish a power law from a lognormal for **any** genre (all p > 0.17).

**Evidence.** Clauset–Shalizi–Newman method: x_min by KS minimisation, α by discrete MLE, goodness of
fit by parametric bootstrap, lognormal comparison by Vuong. See
[`outputs/phase5_powerlaw.md`](outputs/phase5_powerlaw.md).

**Why it can be believed.** It is the standard method for exactly this question, and it is applied at
a common stream length so the comparison is about genre rather than corpus size.

**What it does not establish.** That anything here is power-law distributed — the honest conclusion is
that two heavy-tailed families fit comparably and neither should be claimed. The original
"Zipf-as-DSL detector" (Administrative s=1.746 vs Lexical s=1.114) is withdrawn: that spread of 0.632
collapses to 0.247 under a correct estimator at equal length, and the OLS fit that produced it is
biased with an R² that is not a goodness-of-fit test.

## 5. No evidence of hidden periodic encoding

**Claim.** An equidistant-letter-sequence scan across skips 2–100 in five genres finds 3 nominally
significant results (p < 0.01) out of 495 tests, against ~5 expected by chance.

**Evidence.** 1,000 permutations per skip, `(r+1)/(n+1)` corrected. See
`outputs/compression_findings.md` §3.

**Why it can be believed.** It is a correct permutation test, and being a null result it is the claim
least at risk from the error class that invalidated the RULING analysis.

**What it does not establish.** A Bonferroni-level rejection. At 1,000 permutations the minimum
attainable p is ~0.001, roughly 10× the Bonferroni threshold, so this scan *cannot* return a
Bonferroni-significant result by construction. The original "0 of 495 Bonferroni-significant" framing
overstated its power. The informative comparison is the uncorrected hit rate against chance
expectation, which is what is now reported.

## 6. Provenance-carrying write logs: measured cost, definitional benefit

**Claim.** A sealed-envelope write log answers 5 of 5 audit queries that an anonymous log answers 0
of 5, at +59% bytes, +37 tokens, and +7 µs per write at 250-byte payloads (falling to ~1.4% byte
overhead at 10 KB payloads).

**Evidence.** 100,000 executed writes through both implementations. See
[`benchmarks/RESULTS.md`](benchmarks/RESULTS.md). Asserted in CI on every push.

**Why it can be believed.** It is an engineering measurement, not a statistical inference.

**What it does not establish.** Anything surprising on the capability side. The 0-of-5 is
*definitional* — an anonymous log lacks the fields being queried, so it must fail. The informative
half of this result is the cost side, which is real and measured.

## What is NOT a finding

- **The nine agent primitives** and the reference architecture are design proposals argued from
  cited tablets. The mapping from a Sumerian formula to an agent contract is an argument, not a
  measurement.
- **The three-tier SURFACE → COLUMN → RULING memory design** is an untested proposal. Finding 1 is
  consistent with ruling-aligned chunking being meaningful, but no agent system was built or
  evaluated here.
- **`outputs/FULL_IDEAS.md`** (~158 items) is ideation, preserved for browsability.
- **Five of ten audited regex probes** are unreliable (precision 6–42%) and must not be cited. See
  [`outputs/probe_validation.md`](outputs/probe_validation.md).

## Standing caveats

The corpus is 92%+ administrative, so most findings are about Ur III administrative practice rather
than Sumerian scribal convention generally. `<RULING>` is a transcription artifact reflecting modern
editorial judgement as much as a physical feature of the tablet. Multiple-comparison correction is
applied within analyses (BH across genres in Phase 4, Bonferroni within the ELS scan) but not across
the full set of hypotheses this repository entertains. **None of this has been peer-reviewed, and no
Assyriologist has adjudicated the philology.**
